# Chicken Little — English folktale (retold for graded reading)
# source: traditional, public domain

One fine morning Chicken Little was walking in the farmyard. An acorn fell from a tree and hit her on the tail.

"Oh!" cried Chicken Little. "The sky is falling! I must run and tell the king!" And away she ran as fast as her little legs could go.

On the road she met Henny Penny. "Where are you going, Chicken Little?" asked Henny Penny.

"The sky is falling! A piece of it hit me on the tail. I am going to tell the king," said Chicken Little.

"May I come with you?" asked Henny Penny.

"Yes, come along!" said Chicken Little. So the two of them ran on together.

Soon they met Ducky Daddles. "Where are you going?" he asked.

"The sky is falling! We are going to tell the king," they said.

"May I come too?" asked Ducky Daddles.

"Yes, come along!" they said. So the three of them ran on together.

By and by they met Goosey Poosey, and then Turkey Lurkey. "The sky is falling! We are going to tell the king!" said Chicken Little. So Goosey Poosey and Turkey Lurkey came along too.

At last they came to the dark woods, and there they met Foxy Loxy. He had big sharp teeth and a long bushy tail.

"Where are you all going on this fine day?" asked Foxy Loxy with a smile.

"The sky is falling! We are going to tell the king!" said Chicken Little.

"Oh, I know a short cut to the king's palace," said Foxy Loxy. "Follow me!"

But Foxy Loxy did not take them to the king. He led them round and round, deeper and deeper into the woods, straight towards his own dark den.

Chicken Little looked at the woods and began to think. "This does not look like the way to a palace," she said. Then she saw the fox's den behind the trees, and she saw the bones on the ground.

"Run!" cried Chicken Little. "It is a trick! Run home!"

All the birds flapped and ran as fast as they could. They ran out of the dark woods and all the way back to the farmyard. Foxy Loxy went hungry that day.

That evening Chicken Little sat by the barn and looked up. The sky was blue and bright, right where it had always been.

"The sky is not falling after all," she said. "It was only a little acorn. Next time I will look up and think before I run."
